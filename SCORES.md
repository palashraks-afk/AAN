# Scores

Honest self-grades on the five criteria you set, as of 2026-10-09. They are not adjusted to meet a target. The detailed
reasoning is in `docs/04_honest_grades.md`. Re-grade after the results exist and after the 17 October prior-art search.

| Criterion | Grade (1 to 5) | Why |
|---|:-:|---|
| Super advanced | **5** (design) | Specificity calibration against a 23-trait panel, conditioning, two enrichment methods, permutation null, locus drop, independent GWAS, benchmark, pre-registration. Real risk is execution time, not design. |
| Groundbreaking / impact | **4** | Reach is a 5: over a million US patients, 9 in 10 undiagnosed, about 7% of the research funding its burden would predict. "Changes lives" is unproven: results can direct research, not treat. |
| Strong foundation | **5** | Largest ME/CFS GWAS, peer-reviewed methods (MAGMA, LDSC, Human Brain Cell Atlas, Open Targets), unit tests, controls checked before the real result is read. |
| New and novel | **4** (conditional) | The neuron finding is already reported, so it counts as a replication. Not found in my searches: calibrated specificity, conditioning, subtype contrast at cell-type level, independent-axis count, genetics-versus-imaging convergence, benchmark-validated ranking, in a field that only got its first large GWAS in 2025. Holds only while the 17 Oct re-search finds nothing overlapping; drops to 3 if it does. A 5 is not available. Full evidence: `docs/11_novelty_evidence.md`. |
| Genuinely unique | **4 to 5** (provisional) | No calibrated, multi-method, pre-registered cell-type analysis of ME/CFS found in about 45 searches. Provisional until 17 Oct. |

## What would move a grade
- Novelty to 3 if a paper already runs a calibrated specificity test on ME/CFS.
- Novelty to 5 is not available: the neuron result is a replication and no search proves absence.
- Impact to 5 only if someone acts on the result (trial design, target validation), which is outside these 11 days.
- Uniqueness to 4 if a similar calibrated atlas turns up in the 17 Oct search.

## What judges actually score (so effort goes to the right place)
- Round 1, abstract only: relevance to neuroscience, writing clarity and methods, research potential (innovation, impact, gap).
- Final round: relevance, creativity, interpretation of data **including pitfalls**, report quality with readable figures.
A pre-registered design with controls, honest limits and a clear null-or-positive result scores better than a claim of
"first" that a judge can disprove in thirty seconds.

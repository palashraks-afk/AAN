## 4. Why 4
- A real gap exists in what I could find: no calibrated, conditioned, multi-method, pre-registered cell-type analysis of ME/CFS, no
  independent-axis count, no genetics-versus-imaging convergence test, no benchmark-validated target ranking.
- The field itself is young (first large ME/CFS GWAS 2025) and heavily under-researched, so the direction is relatively unexplored.
- The design is built to give a reportable answer either way: a specific axis, or the clear null that ME/CFS looks like any
  polygenic brain trait.

## 5. Conditions that keep it at 4, and what drops it to 3
Keeps it at 4:
1. The 17 October search finds no calibrated specificity analysis for ME/CFS.
2. The control check passes and the layers actually run (a layer that cannot run is removed from the claim).
3. The abstract describes the new part exactly as in section 6 and never uses "first".
Drops it to 3 if:
- A paper, preprint or the published DecodeME paper already runs a panel-calibrated cell-type specificity test for ME/CFS, or
- the final DecodeME paper adds cell-type work that covers most rows of section 1.
Stays below 5 regardless: the neuron result is a replication, and no database search proves absence.

## 6. How to word it (use these, not stronger words)
- "ME/CFS is a young and under-researched field; the first large genome-wide study appeared in 2025."
- "Earlier work, including the DecodeME team's, points to neurons. I tested whether that neuronal signal is specific to ME/CFS or is
  shared by other polygenic brain traits, using a pre-registered comparison panel."
- "To my knowledge this specificity test has not been reported for ME/CFS (searched PubMed, bioRxiv, medRxiv on [date])."
- Do not write: "first", "never done", "groundbreaking", "novel discovery" before the result exists.

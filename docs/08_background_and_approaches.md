# Background statistics and how the research approaches compare

Compiled 2026-10-08 from searches. Numbers are quoted as the sources give them; where sources disagree both are shown.
Advocacy and company pages are marked and should not be cited as primary evidence.

## 1. The size of the problem

| Fact | Figure | Source and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| People with ME/CFS in the US (CDC) | up to 3.3 million; over 9 in 10 never diagnosed by a doctor | CDC, https://www.cdc.gov/me-cfs/about/index.html |
| Earlier academic range | 836,000 to 2.5 million (National Academy of Medicine 2015); about 1.5 million (Jason and Mirin update, adults 1.09 M plus children 0.40 M) | Mirin et al. 2020 and related papers; counts depend on case definition |
| Projection with Long COVID | 5 to 9 million, a projection, not a count | Jason and Mirin 2022 update |
| Annual cost to the US economy | $18 to $51 billion (CDC); $36 to $51 billion (Jason and Mirin) | CDC; NCHS Data Brief 488 (Dec 2023) |
| Funding against burden | ME/CFS receives about 7% of the NIH funding its disease burden would predict, the lowest ratio in that analysis (some versions say under 1% of the matching share) | Mirin, Dimmock and Jason 2020, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/WOR-203173 |
| NIH spending in 2019 | about $15 million for ME/CFS against over $200 million each for Parkinson's disease and schizophrenia; those diseases had 4 to 22 times more registered trials | ME/CFS Science analysis (advocacy group; check against NIH RePORTER before quoting) |

| Sex | about 80% of ME/CFS patients are female; 84% of DecodeME cases | DecodeME preprint |

## 2. What is already known about the nervous system in ME/CFS (context for the genetics)
- Brainstem and hypothalamus: a 1995 perfusion study found brainstem hypoperfusion not seen in depression or epilepsy;
  MRI work reports brainstem and hypothalamic abnormalities; reviews propose orexin and hypothalamic dysfunction
  (hypotheses).
- Neuroinflammation: PET with the 11C-PK11195 tracer (2014, 9 patients and 10 controls) found raised binding in widespread
  areas including hippocampus, amygdala, thalamus, midbrain and pons; a TSPO-PET study reported raised signal in the
  putamen (conference abstract). A newer TSPO study found no group difference, and tracer binding depends on genotype.
- Autonomic function: postural tachycardia was more common in ME/CFS (46% against 19% of controls in one small study); a
  meta-analysis found higher resting and orthostatic heart rate.
- A 2023 meta-analysis of 65 imaging studies found cortical changes most often reported but pooled hypoactivity in insula
  and thalamus. Neuroimaging findings overall are not consistent between studies.

## 3. How the main research approaches compare

| Approach | What it can tell us | Typical sample | Main weakness | Where this project fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Genome-wide association (DecodeME) | Inherited risk, causal direction (genes come before illness) | 15,579 cases, 259,909 controls | Says nothing about current disease state; controls from UK Biobank (healthy-volunteer bias); European ancestry | The data this project uses |
| Neuroimaging (fMRI, PET, MRI) | Where the living brain differs | tens of patients (for example 26 cases and 18 controls) | Small samples; findings do not replicate; brain-wide studies in other diseases need hundreds per group | Independent evidence for the regions genetics points to (E1) |
| Immune profiling (cytokines, single-cell) | Blood-cell differences | tens to hundreds; one multicentre study had 298 | No robust, reproducible biomarker; sex and batch confound results | Genetics tests whether signal is in immune or neural cells |
| Metabolomics | Chemical state of blood | 26 to 52 patients in several studies | No metabolite consistently altered; power weakened by many metabolites and few people | Not used |
| Animal models | Mechanism and treatment tests | lab cohorts | Induced-inflammation models do not reproduce the human illness; protocols not standardised | Candidate genes could later be tested here |
| This project: cell-type mapping of GWAS signal | Which cell types the inherited risk acts through | the same 15,579 cases | Statistical association, three-donor atlas, CNS only | Adds cellular resolution to GWAS and checks it against the other approaches |

Take-away for the introduction: GWAS is the only ME/CFS approach with sample sizes in the tens of thousands. Everything
else has the same pattern of small cohorts, mixed case definitions and weak replication. A genetic signal that also agrees
with independent imaging and physiology is therefore worth more than any one of them alone, and a genetic signal that
does not agree is worth reporting.
